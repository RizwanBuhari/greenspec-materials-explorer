Understanding impact

Three ways to read a material.

Each material in the library is scored on three dimensions. Here is what they mean and how to use them.

Illustrative demo data. Scores are fictional examples, not verified measurements or a basis for specification.

Carbon score

Lower is better

The relative greenhouse-gas impact of producing the material: extraction, processing and manufacturing. A lower score means less climate impact per unit of material.

Recyclability score

Higher is better

The potential for the material to be recovered and made into new material at end of life. It depends on collection, sorting and whether the material keeps its quality.

Energy score

Lower is better

The relative energy demand of producing the material. High-temperature processes such as smelting, melting and firing push this score up.

Compare the same function

Compare equal function and service life, not just material names. A material that lasts twice as long, or can be repaired, can outperform one with better scores on paper. These example indices have no physical units or verified dataset; they are not lifecycle assessment results.
